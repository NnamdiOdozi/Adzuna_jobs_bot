# Four-day Jev replay with revised instructions

Run on 22 November 2026 at approximately 21:29 GMT. The user authorised replaying
the four saved daily batches. Jev was called; Adzuna and JSearch were not.
No emails were sent, and no sent-job or delivery history was changed.

## Result in plain English

The revisions fix several examples we wanted to fix, but introduce other weak
decisions. They are a partial improvement, not a demonstrated overall improvement.
I would keep the language-profile and salary-provenance fixes, but would not
describe the revised shared policy as ready merely because the tests pass.

## What to do with these results

Keep the honest salary labels and English/Vietnamese profile. Those changes have
clear purposes independent of whether this prompt is better overall.

For the prompt, the next useful change would be to replace the accumulated
instructions with shorter, clearly ordered category rules. In particular:
distinguish a clear Accounts Assistant title from a generic Analyst title; treat
unknown required eligibility as unknown; and require actual job evidence rather
than recruitment publicity. This replay shows that adding those statements to
the existing prose did not make Jev follow them consistently.

Do not automatically add another model, impose a probability cutoff, or exclude
all associates, legal employers or temporary jobs. These would create other
missed opportunities. If a further revision is authorised, first check it on the
specific problem pairs above before spending on another full four-day replay.
No further paid trial or prompt edit was made after these results.

## How the comparison was controlled

The replay used the existing `jobs_bot.jev_ranking.rank_jobs` adapter and model
`jev-1.14.0`. Each source day's saved vacancies, order, prior sent-job keys and
search settings were reused. The assessment date was held at the original day.
The same 199 IDs were assessed in both versions. One previously seen job on
19 November remained excluded before Jev, as in the original run.

The changed inputs were the revised candidate profile and shared policy. Salary
metadata preservation does not alter the advert fields sent to Jev. These are
single trials on different dates, so natural model/service variation cannot be
separated from the policy changes. No claim of measured accuracy, calibration
or statistical significance is made. Most source descriptions are only 500
characters; no prompt can recover requirements that are absent from them.

The four days made twelve successful Jev requests, without retries, for 132,932
reported input tokens and 10,518 output tokens. The earlier outputs reported
104,332 input tokens and 10,500 output tokens. Input usage increased by about
27%, consistent with the longer policy. This is token usage, not an invoice or
a verified current price.
